# Media Minder Design Bible — Approved Baseline

## Mission
Media Minder quietly guides people toward exceptional entertainment.

## Product principles
1. Recommendations First.
2. Editorial Over Algorithm. *(Beta exception, Aug 2026: "why" copy for the expanded catalog is AI-drafted, not hand-written, to unblock the friends & family beta. Simon reserves the right to revert to hand-written copy if the AI voice reads as generic.)*
3. Reduce Friction.
4. Entertainment Takes Center Stage.
5. Quiet Confidence.
6. Beautiful Restraint.
7. One Page. One Purpose.
8. One Component. One Responsibility.
9. No Clutter.
10. Trust the User.
11. The Best Feature Is the One Nobody Notices.

## Product personality
Intelligent, calm, thoughtful, refined, editorial, warm, trustworthy.

## Visual language
Atomic Age modern; pastel 1950s; classic broadcast television influence; retro without kitsch.

## MM Select
Select, Gold, Silver. Select is the top tier, reserved for a standout movie pick (the star badge); Gold is intentionally scarce.

## Navigation
Landing, Tonight, Recommendations, Watchlist, Calendar, Premieres, Movies, Franchises, My Reviews, Settings, Search. No Discover page.

## Core UX decisions
- Watch progress is expressed as episode counts, not percentages.
- Franchises emphasize the most imminent universe activity.
- Search is a utility, not a discovery feed.
- Hidden gems are used only when they are genuinely strong.
- Platforms remain visible; entertainment remains the star.
- Seasonal treatments should be subtle and complementary.

\n## Brand Art
- The MM logo uses a strong MM monogram with a broadcast test-pattern motif in negative space.
- MM Select badges use one entertainment/broadcast visual language across Select, Gold and Silver.
- Badge artwork must remain compact so recommendation cards do not become cluttered.
## Card Hierarchy (Hero → Secondary → Compact → Wildcard)
On any page presenting a ranked list of recommendations, exactly one item receives full emphasis. Every item after it steps down in visual weight. This keeps a page feeling calm and directional instead of repeating the same loud treatment down the length of the screen.

Tier is a position in the hierarchy, independent of what kind of card it is (MM Select, Tonight, Watching, Premiere). A page assigns tier by position, not by content type.

1. Hero — the single top pick on the page. Largest art panel and title. Reserved for exactly one card per page.
2. Secondary — the next pick. Same banner and detail treatment as Hero, one size down.
3. Compact — remaining ranked picks. Smaller art panel and title, tightened spacing. Used for lists of three or more.
4. Wildcard — the one pick outside the ranked/scored set (e.g. Recommendations' "One Wildcard" slot). Needs its own visual treatment, distinct from the ranked tiers — not yet designed. Until a Canva pass defines it, leave wildcard slots on their current treatment rather than forcing them into a ranked-looking tier.

Rule of thumb: one Hero, then step down. A page should never show two Hero-weight cards back to back.

Where this applies:
- Tonight: item 1 = Hero, item 2 = Secondary, items 3-4 = Compact.
- Recommendations: the MM Select pick = Hero, the rest of the Gold row = Compact, wildcard = pending design.
- Premieres: naturally a list — all items use Compact.
- Home/Landing: separate layout family (dashboard, not a stacked card list), scoped separately against the Ideal Landing reference.
